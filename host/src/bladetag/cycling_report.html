Compare measures

Each dot is a five-second moving median in the selected time range; colour runs from early (green) to late (orange). HR can lag effort. Speed also changes with wind and slope. Estimated metres/turn uses speed divided by estimated cadence, so its link with speed is partly mathematical.

How behavior and measurements vary together

Separate 30-second moving blocks reduce repeated counting of overlapping cadence windows. Spearman r describes whether two measures rise together (positive) or move in opposite directions (negative). Before/after-break rows help expose changes hidden in the whole-ride average.

Ride portionMeasuresBlocksSpearman r

These are associations, not effects of changing your behavior. Adjacent blocks still depend on each other; no significance test is claimed. HR lags effort, and wind, slope, route and pacing affect these comparisons. Metres per turn is excluded because it is calculated from speed and cadence.

What to try: Compare a gentler first five minutes with your usual start on repeat rides. To test cadence, alternate comfortable cadence choices over the same uninterrupted stretch, in the same direction, at similar speed; repeat in reverse order and compare HR after it settles. Record perceived effort too. This ride alone cannot identify your most efficient cadence. Keep necessary slowing for bends and other people; compare how gradually you regain speed afterward.
