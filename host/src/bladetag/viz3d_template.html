How this is worked out

One tag sees one skate. A stroke runs from the wheels touching down to the next touch-down, found from road vibration. Within each stroke the acceleration is integrated twice and the skater's own constant speed is taken out, so the path shows the skate relative to you, the way a camera riding alongside would see it.

Wheels only roll along the skate, so while they're down the skate must point where it's going. That fixes which way the tag sits on the skate.

Sideways sweep includes your body swaying. Heights stay small because the wheels are pinned to the road while they're down, and a low recovery barely leaves it.
